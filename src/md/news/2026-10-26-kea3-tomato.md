Here we present a collaboration effort, with the mechanistic-kinetic model developed by our PhD student Quang Huy Nguyen, and the experimental efforts from the group of Prof. Ute Armbruster, led by Dr. Thekla von Bismarck. 

In this project, we investigated the activity of KEA3, a potassium-proton exchanger on the thylakoid lumen with significant role in NPQ relaxation, in tomato (_Solanum lycopersicum_). While havings the potential to improve photosnthesis efficient via enhanced NPQ relaxation kinetics, overexpression of KEA3 in tomato do not shows any significant impacts on carbon fixation rate, biomass yield and drought reponses.

We compared the KEA3 knock-out (KO) mutants of the model plant _Arabidopsis thaliana_ and tomato,  and we showed that lacks of KEA3 have diffent impacts on the two species. Via species-specific odinary differential equations (ODEs)-based kinetics model of NPQ, we proposed that it is not KEA3 that underlies the cross-species differences, but rather the kinetics of the PsbS protein, a main components of NPQ in higher plants. Overall, the project implies the potential of applying mechanistic models to untangle NPQ kinetics at different plant species, which can have potential impact on translating the molecular knowledge from model plants to crop plants.

The manuscript is now online at BioRxiv: [https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.13.758443](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.13.758443). An online version of the model can also be found at: [https://cpbl.rwth-aachen.de/mxl-web/models/kea3-tomato](https://cpbl.rwth-aachen.de/mxl-web/models/kea3-tomato)
