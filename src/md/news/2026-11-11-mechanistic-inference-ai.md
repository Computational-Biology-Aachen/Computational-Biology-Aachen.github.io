We are happy to share our new perspective preprint "When Everyone Can Build a Model: Mechanistic Inference in the Age of AI", now available on arXiv. The work is a collaboration between our group and Prof. Adèle H. Ribeiro (Causal and Explainable Artificial Intelligence in Life Sciences), both part of the Center for Computational Life Sciences at RWTH Aachen University.

Deep learning and AI-assisted code generation have made it easier than ever for domain experts to build semi-mechanistic, "grey-box" models of biological systems. Building a model has become easy, but interpreting it has not. A model that predicts well does not prove that its inferred mechanism is the real one, and the learned components can quietly absorb errors from the mechanistic part. Many biological models are also underdetermined: several different models can fit the same data equally well.

We argue that this ambiguity has two sources: the choice of which variables influence which others, and the choice of the response function describing each mechanism. Causal inference tackles the first by stating which relationships and intervention effects can actually be identified from data. Ensemble analyses tackle the second by quantifying the uncertainty and the range of behaviours that equally good models allow. Taken together, they show what the data support, what remains open, and which experiment would settle the question.

The preprint can be found here: [https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.41454](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.41454)
